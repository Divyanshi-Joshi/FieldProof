SECTION 1 — INTERPRETATION, DEFINITIONS, AND GOVERNING JURISPRUDENCE
================================================================================

CL-1.1: PRIMARY DEFINITION OF INDEX PERFORMANCE & RAINFALL TRIGGERING
(Page 3, Paragraph 1.1)
The Insurer agrees to indemnify the Insured Smallholder Farmer against cumulative
seasonal precipitation deficiency below the actuarially established contract threshold.
The Primary Index shall trigger automatically (index_pays = TRUE) when cumulative
seasonal precipitation recorded across the specified Dekadal Window falls strictly
below the 30th percentile (P30) of the long-term historical distribution (1991–2020 ERA5
reanalysis and UCSB-CHG CHIRPS daily high-resolution pentads). Where seasonal rainfall
equals or exceeds the P30 threshold (P >= 290.0 mm for Marathwada rain-shadow districts), the Primary
Index shall register an outcome of Non-Trigger (index_pays = FALSE).

CL-1.2: DEFINITION OF BASIS RISK
(Page 4, Paragraph 1.2)
"Basis Risk" is legally defined as any material divergence between the area-yield or
gridded index determination and the actual agronomic loss suffered at the individual
farm plot. Basis risk encompasses two distinct statutory classes:
  (a) Downside Basis Risk (Type II Basis Risk / Basis Risk Gap): Where the index fails
      to trigger despite severe vegetative mortality at the plot centroid;
  (b) Upside Basis Risk (Type I Basis Risk / Overpayment): Where the index triggers a
      payout to an insured plot that experienced normal or surplus vegetative yield.

CL-1.3: PREVALENCE OF RECONCILIATION PROTOCOL
(Page 5, Paragraph 1.3)
To remedy the inherent unfairness of conventional index insurance, this policy enacts
the FieldProof Multi-Sensor Reconciliation Protocol. The Insurer shall maintain an active
ground-and-orbit audit mechanism to detect, classify, and escalate all instances of
basis risk prior to final policy settlement.

SECTION 2 — CONFIRMED PAYOUT AND FAST-TRACK INDEMNIFICATION
================================================================================

CL-2.1: CONFIRMED PAYOUT FAST-TRACK PROTOCOL
(Page 7, Paragraph 2.1)
Where the Primary Rainfall Index triggers (index_pays = TRUE) AND independent ground-level
photographic evidence confirms crop damage at MODERATE or SEVERE intensity, the claim
shall be classified as a CONFIRMED PAYOUT.
  (a) The claim is immediately routed to the human loss adjuster with a pre-populated
      fast-track authorization brief;
  (b) Underwriting guidelines permit expedited approval within twenty-four (24) hours
      of digital intake;
  (c) Payout calculation proceeds according to the statutory sum insured per hectare (₹25,000 INR/ha).

CL-2.2: POSSIBLE OVERPAYMENT MITIGATION AND REVIEW
(Page 8, Paragraph 2.2)
Where the Primary Rainfall Index triggers (index_pays = TRUE) but photographic evidence
graded by the calibrated perception engine verifies NONE or MILD damage (healthy crop canopy):
  (a) Automated payout disbursement is strictly halted;
  (b) The claim is flagged as a POSSIBLE OVERPAYMENT;
  (c) A certified agricultural surveyor or adjuster must review the discrepancy within
      five (5) business days to determine whether micro-climate, supplemental irrigation,
      or soil moisture retention explains the crop's survival.

SECTION 3 — GROUND EVIDENCE ASSESSMENT & CONCEPT-BOTTLENECK CONSTRAINTS
================================================================================

CL-3.1: CONCEPT-BOTTLENECK AGRONOMIC RUBRIC
(Page 11, Paragraph 3.1)
Artificial intelligence perception systems employed by the Insurer are legally restricted
from outputting direct monetary valuations, loss percentages, or subjective damage scores.
AI vision models must evaluate claims exclusively through an explainable concept-bottleneck
vector scoring four physical agronomic indicators:
  (i)   Leaf Rolling (0 to 3): Degree of inward longitudinal curled foliage;
  (ii)  Canopy Senescence (0 to 3): Fractional necrosis/yellowing of crop stand;
  (iii) Stunting (0 to 3): Relative stand height deficit versus growth stage norm;
  (iv)  Soil Cracking (Boolean): Presence of polygonal desiccation fissures.
All mappings from the sign vector to severity bands (NONE, MILD, MODERATE, SEVERE) must
be executed by verified, deterministic pure Java code.

CL-3.2: MULTI-PERMUTATION REFERENCE ANCHOR CALIBRATION
(Page 12, Paragraph 3.2)
To eliminate hardware camera bias, white balance distortion, and prompt drift, every
model inference must prepend three fixed visual reference anchors (healthy=0, moderate=2,
severe=3) sourced from the vetted development archive. Anchor permutation order must be
shuffled across grading passes to prevent positional bias.

SECTION 4 — BASIS RISK ESCALATION & GAP RESOLUTION PROTOCOL
================================================================================

CL-4.1: STATUTORY BASIS-RISK ESCALATION MANDATE
(Page 16, Paragraph 4.1)
When the Primary Index fails to trigger (index_pays = FALSE) but the farmer files a
contemporaneous claim supported by ground imagery, the Insurer is bound by statutory
mandate to evaluate satellite orbital telemetry and phenological timing before issuing
any denial of indemnity.

CL-4.2: PHENOLOGICAL CRITICAL-STAGE DROUGHT ESCALATION
(Page 18, Paragraph 4.2)
Where the Primary Index does NOT trigger (e.g. cumulative rainfall exceeded 290 mm),
a claim shall be classified as a PHENOLOGICAL BASIS-RISK GAP and escalated for full payout when:
  (a) Ground photos confirm MODERATE or SEVERE drought damage;
  (b) Google Earth Engine Sentinel-2 multi-spectral NDVI anomaly exhibits verified
      vegetative stress (Z <= -1.5 relative to the 3-year baseline); AND
  (c) CHIRPS daily precipitation records confirm a continuous dry spell exceeding
      fourteen (14) consecutive days overlapping the crop's critical reproductive
      or flowering phenological window.
Under Clause 4.2, the temporal distribution of rainfall supersedes aggregate volume.

CL-4.2.1: PREVENTED SOWING / PLANTING / GERMINATION RISK
(PMFBY Operational Guidelines §4.2.1, Page 19, Paragraph 4.2.1)
In case the majority of the insured farmers of an administrative unit (village / mandal / block),
having intent to sow/plant and incurred expenditure for the purpose, are prevented from
sowing/planting/germination due to adverse weather conditions (deficit rainfall or prolonged
monsoon delay):
  (a) Farmers shall be eligible for an indemnity payout up to a maximum of 25% of the Sum Insured;
  (b) The policy contract shall stand terminated upon disbursement of the prevented sowing indemnity;
  (c) Assessment relies on State Government seasonal rainfall triggers, automatic weather stations (AWS),
      and Earth Engine Sentinel-1 SAR soil moisture verification.

CL-4.3: SPATIAL BASIS-RISK ESCALATION (TOPOGRAPHIC / MICRO-CLIMATIC DEFICIT)
(Page 21, Paragraph 4.3)
Where the Primary Index does NOT trigger, but:
  (a) Ground photos confirm MODERATE or SEVERE drought damage;
  (b) Google Earth Engine Sentinel-2 NDVI anomaly verifies stress (Z <= -1.5);
  (c) No dry spell overlap occurred during flowering, BUT plot-level CHIRPS rainfall
      was severely depressed relative to the county weather station aggregate (low local rain);
The claim shall be classified as a SPATIAL BASIS-RISK GAP and escalated for adjuster sign-off.

SECTION 5 — EVIDENCE CONFLICTS, LOCALIZED CALAMITIES & MID-SEASON ADVERSITY
================================================================================

CL-5.1: EVIDENCE CONFLICT HOLD PROTOCOL
(Page 24, Paragraph 5.1)
Where ground photographs exhibit MODERATE or SEVERE damage, but orbital satellite
telemetry (Google Earth Engine Sentinel-2 L2A) does NOT confirm vegetative stress (Z > -1.5):
  (a) The claim must NOT be denied summarily;
  (b) The claim is placed on an EVIDENCE CONFLICT HOLD;
  (c) The automated system prompts the farmer via SMS/WhatsApp to submit two (2) supplementary
      wide-angle plot photos within forty-eight (48) hours;
  (d) If conflicting signals persist, the claim is assigned to an in-person field adjuster.

CL-5.3: MID-SEASON ADVERSITY ON-ACCOUNT RELIEF
(PMFBY Operational Guidelines §5.3, Page 25, Paragraph 5.3)
Where adverse seasonal conditions (severe drought, dry spell, flood, unseasonal widespread rainfall)
occur during the cropping season, and expected crop yield is projected to drop below 50% of the
historical threshold yield:
  (a) An on-account payment of up to 25% of the likely claim amount shall be disbursed immediately
      to provide liquidity to smallholders;
  (b) The on-account payment is adjusted against the final claim settlement calculated after harvest.

CL-5.4: LOCALIZED CALAMITIES (HAILSTORM, INUNDATION, CLOUDBURST, LANDSLIDE)
(PMFBY Operational Guidelines §5.4, Page 26, Paragraph 5.4)
Loss or damage resulting from the occurrence of identified localized risks of hailstorm,
landslide, inundation, cloudburst, and natural fire due to lightning affecting isolated farms:
  (a) Assessment is carried out at the individual affected insured farm plot level;
  (b) The insured farmer must notify the Insurer or nodal bank within seventy-two (72) hours of the peril;
  (c) Photographic evidence of submerged crop stands or hail-defoliated canopy is corroborated with
      Sentinel-1 SAR inundation backscatter (VV/VH dual-pol cross-ratio drop) and local rain gauges.

SECTION 6 — NON-WEATHER PERIL EXCLUSIONS & ADVISORY TRANSITION
================================================================================

CL-6.1: EXCLUSION OF BIOTIC PERILS (PEST, FUNGAL, AND BACTERIAL INFESTATION)
(Page 28, Paragraph 6.1)
Parametric weather-index insurance strictly covers abiotic meteorological perils (drought,
excess rain, unseasonal frost).
  (a) Where Gemini Vision identifies primary damage caused by Spodoptera frugiperda
      (Fall Armyworm), Busseola fusca (Maize Stalk Borer), Turcicum leaf blight, or
      Maize Lethal Necrosis Disease (MLND), indemnity under the weather index is EXCLUDED;
  (b) The claim is classified as NON_WEATHER_LOSS;
  (c) The system routes the findings to the Ministry of Agriculture Extension Advisory,
      providing the farmer with immediate agronomic pest-management recommendations.

CL-6.2: SOIL NUTRIENT DEFICIENCY AND AGRONOMIC MISMANAGEMENT
(Page 30, Paragraph 6.2)
Crop stunting resulting demonstrably from severe nitrogen chlorosis, striga weed infestation,
or lack of basal fertilizer without concomitant drought signs is excluded under Clause 6.1.

SECTION 7 — HUMAN ADJUSTER PREROGATIVE & TWO-PASS SAFETY GATES
================================================================================

CL-7.1: INVIOLABLE HUMAN SIGN-OFF MANDATE
(Page 34, Paragraph 7.1)
No claim may be finalized, disbursed, or denied solely by an algorithmic or AI process.
The AI system acts strictly as an evidentiary synthesizer and draft brief generator.
Every final claim outcome must be formally endorsed by an accredited human loss adjuster.

CL-7.2: TWO-PASS AGREEMENT SAFETY GATE AND ABSTENTION
(Page 36, Paragraph 7.2)
Every claim photo bundle must undergo two independent inferences with permuted visual
calibration anchors (Pass 1 and Pass 2).
  (a) If Pass 1 and Pass 2 yield divergent severity bands (e.g. Pass 1 = MODERATE,
      Pass 2 = MILD), the system is prohibited from guessing or averaging;
  (b) The system outputs ABSTAIN;
  (c) The claim is routed to a human adjuster with an explicit notification that model
      uncertainty exceeded the contractual tolerance threshold.

CL-7.1.4: POST-HARVEST LOSSES UNDER CUT & SPREAD CONDITION
(PMFBY Operational Guidelines §7.1.4, Page 37, Paragraph 7.1.4)
Coverage is available for harvested crops kept in 'cut and spread' or small bundled condition in
the open field to dry, for a maximum period of fourteen (14) days from the harvesting date:
  (a) Covered perils include unseasonal rainfall, cyclonic rain, and hailstorm across all notified districts;
  (b) Assessment of post-harvest loss is conducted on an individual plot basis;
  (c) The farmer must provide geotagged photos of the cut harvest in the field showing water-logging,
      grain germination/sprouting, or mold discoloration within 72 hours of damage occurrence.

SECTION 8 — FORENSIC IMAGE INTEGRITY AND PLANT-FRAUD PREVENTION
================================================================================

CL-8.1: IMAGE INTEGRITY SCREENING AND HOLD
(Page 40, Paragraph 8.1)
Prior to model perception grading, all uploaded imagery must pass automated cryptographic
and digital forensic integrity filters:
  (i)   Intra-Claim Duplicate Detection (pHash/dHash Hamming distance <= 10);
  (ii)  Cross-Claim Historical Re-use (lookup against immutable image registry);
  (iii) Screen Recapture & Moiré Analysis (detection of computer/mobile displays);
  (iv)  Multi-Plot Heterogeneity (detection of inconsistent soil, horizon, or crop variety);
  (v)   EXIF Boundary Verification (timestamp freshness and Indian agricultural GPS bounding box: Lat 8.0°N–35.0°N, Lon 68.0°E–97.0°E).
Failure of any check triggers an INTEGRITY_HOLD, suspending automated recommendations.

SECTION 9 — SATELLITE ORBITAL VERIFICATION PROTOCOL (GOOGLE EARTH ENGINE)
================================================================================

CL-9.1: COPERNICUS SENTINEL-2 SURFACE REFLECTANCE STANDARD
(Page 43, Paragraph 9.1)
Orbital verification must employ Sentinel-2 Level-2A Bottom-of-Atmosphere (BOA) Surface
Reflectance (COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED) filtered through the Scene Classification Layer
(SCL) to eliminate cloud and cirrus pixels (cloud_mask < 20%). The Normalized Difference
Vegetation Index (NDVI = (B8 - B4) / (B8 + B4)) computed across the 10-meter plot pixel
must be standardized against the 3-year harmonic seasonal mean.

CL-9.2: CHIRPS DAILY PRECIPITATION DYNAMICS
(Page 45, Paragraph 9.2)
Precipitation telemetry must draw from UCSB Climate Hazards Center CHIRPS Daily (0.05° ~5.3 km)
satellite-gauge blended series. Consecutive dry days are defined as daily precipitation
below 2.5 mm occurring within the designated dekadal vegetative cycle.
